US$50,000 to US$1,000,000

My central estimate is about US$250,000 of additional industry value, in 2026 dollars, over three effective years of use. The main benefit is specialist research capacity freed by reducing interpolation artefacts, field-table preparation and repeated analysis.

The [pull request](https://github.com/proximafusion/vmecpp/pull/819) replaces linear interpolation of external magnetic-field tables with cubic interpolation in free-boundary plasma simulations. Its earlier benchmarks report 16 to 100 times smaller field-table errors with a 51 by 51 cubic table than a 101 by 101 linear table, against a finer reference. That means roughly a quarter as many table entries at unchanged toroidal resolution. These are reported results from an earlier revision, not independent measurements of the final patch. The largest reported original-grid median slowdown was 6.1%, and the difficult-case convergence rate was unchanged. The gain is accuracy and potentially cheaper table preparation; faster equilibrium solves are not established.

There is a credible but small market for this improvement. [Proxima already uses VMEC++ for stellarator design](https://www.proximafusion.com/press-news/introducing-vmecpp-open-source-software-for-fusion-research). For scale, the [FIA's 2024 survey](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/2024-annual-global-fusion-industry-report.pdf) counted eight stellarator companies. I assume eventual use by 5 to 20 industrial and supporting research teams, not a measured installation count. Assume each gains the equivalent of 40 to 120 specialist hours annually, net of revalidation and additional computing costs. I value those hours at US$125 to US$175 including overheads, using the [US physicist median salary of US$172,250 in May 2025](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/physicists-and-astronomers.htm) as a rough labour-value anchor.

Those assumptions imply a conditional benefit of **5 to 20 teams × 40 to 120 hours × 3 years × US$125 to US$175 = US$75,000 to US$1.26 million**. I assign a **65% to 80% probability** of sustained use producing savings at the respective scales, giving approximately **US$49,000 to US$1.01 million** in expected value. The central calculation is 75% × 10 teams × 80 hours × 3 years × US$140 = US$252,000. These probabilities and savings are judgement calls. The change is merged and enabled by default, with polynomial and equilibrium regression tests supporting useful deployment; actual workflow savings remain unmeasured.

The three-year allowance limits credit for a standard numerical improvement that others could implement. Users can also refine existing tables or use [direct field evaluation](https://proximafusion.github.io/vmecpp/tutorials/free_boundary.html). Fixed-boundary calculations and other sources of modelling error receive no demonstrated benefit. Wider effects on reactor design remain unquantified.
